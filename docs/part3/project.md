# Part 3 project proposal: York University Station ventilation and air quality upgrade

Option A (an improvement to the York University subway station platform, TTC Line 1). This is the project description given, word for word, to both the Project Evaluation Platform and the AI agent.

---

Our project focuses on improving the ventilation and air quality at York University Subway Station on TTC Line 1. Since many students rely on the subway to travel to and from campus every day, we want to make the station more comfortable, especially during busy hours when the platforms become crowded.

We propose upgrading the existing ventilation system by installing energy-efficient fans, air filtration equipment and sensors that measure temperature, humidity, air quality and the number of passengers on the platform. These sensors would allow the system to automatically adjust the airflow based on the conditions inside the station instead of operating at the same speed throughout the day.

The project would be owned and managed by the Toronto Transit Commission (TTC) and would cover the entire platform level of York University Station. The estimated budget is $3 million, and the project is expected to take approximately 12 months, including design, equipment installation and testing. These are preliminary estimates for the proposed project.

The project mainly involves mechanical engineering, particularly HVAC systems, fluid mechanics, heat transfer and energy efficiency. Electrical and control engineering would also be needed to install the sensors and connect them to the ventilation system.

The people who would benefit from this project include students, university staff, daily commuters and TTC employees. The main goal is to provide a more comfortable environment while reducing unnecessary energy consumption.

Some challenges that may arise include construction disruptions, maintenance costs and compatibility with the existing ventilation and emergency systems. Before moving forward, engineers would need to assess the current station conditions, conduct safety assessments and ensure that the proposed system meets Ontario's engineering, safety and environmental requirements.
